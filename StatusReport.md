The corresponding unemployment gap averaged 0.06 points, even though its direction varied across years. The [comparison summary](results/us_vs_peers_summary.csv) records the sample sizes and period results.

The [correlation table](results/correlations.csv) shows a near-zero association between GDP growth and the unemployment rate itself (r = -0.03) and a negative association with the annual change in unemployment (r = -0.60). The [regression results](results/okun_regression.csv) compare pooled estimates with country fixed effects and check sensitivity to excluding 2020–2021.

## Challenges

- **Missing and unpublished observations.** The files end in 2025, and U.S. inflation is blank for that year. We kept the 2025 U.S. record because its other two indicators are available. We omitted that year only from the U.S. inflation comparison and computed both the U.S. and peer inflation averages on the same 25 years.
- **Comparability across exports.** Each raw file stores one year per column, and country names can vary across sources. Reshaping the files and using country codes with years gave us a stable linkage key. Checking unique keys and the expected 130 rows helps detect accidental duplicates or dropped records during integration.
- **Interpreting the results.** Annual changes in unemployment require a prior observation, so the first year for each country is omitted from those calculations. Because 2020 and 2021 brought unusually large shifts, we also reran the relationship analysis without those years. The correlation with unemployment changes remained negative (r = -0.61). These results describe associations in the five-country panel, and differences in policy or economic conditions may also explain the patterns.
